302,Poliomyelitis and Post-Polio,"Poliomyelitis, commonly known as polio, is an infectious disease caused by the poliovirus. It was once very common in the United States and worldwide, causing severe illness in thousands of people each year. Most people infected with the poliovirus have no signs of illness and are never aware they have been infected. However, for others, the poliovirus causes symptoms which typically start with fever, tiredness, nausea and headache. In a small number of cases, the virus enters the central nervous system and damages the nerve cells that activate muscles, which can lead to paralysis.

Due to successful immunization efforts, polio is now very rare. However, for some who had polio in the past, a condition known as post-polio syndrome (PPS) can occur many years later. Symptoms of PPS include progressive muscle weakness, fatigue, pain in the muscles and joints, and difficulty breathing and swallowing. There is currently no cure for PPS, but the symptoms can be managed with physical therapy and lifestyle changes. 

The poliovirus is transmitted through contaminated water or food, or contact with an infected person. To prevent polio, vaccination is essential. The inactivated poliovirus vaccine (IPV) and the oral poliovirus vaccine (OPV) are the two vaccines used worldwide to fight polio, with IPV being the only one used in the United States."
