The chart helps organizations and individuals to prioritize problems or opportunities by visually displaying the relative frequency of occurrences, showing which issues are most critical.

1. **Bar Graph**: The chart consists of bars, where the height of each bar represents the frequency or impact of a particular category. The bars are arranged in descending order of frequency, from left to right.
2. **Line**: A line (often called the ""Pareto line"") is also included in the chart. It represents the cumulative percentage of the total frequency or impact. The line starts at the top of the first bar and moves upward as it connects the tops of subsequent bars.
3. **Categories**: The categories or issues are listed along the horizontal axis. These could be types of defects, causes of problems, or any other factors being analyzed.
4. **Cumulative Percentage**: The vertical axis typically represents the cumulative percentage, showing the total contribution of each category to the overall problem or opportunity.

### How to Create a Pareto Chart:
1. **Collect Data**: Gather data on the specific categories or issues you want to analyze.
2. **Categorize**: Organize the data into categories.
3. **Sort**: Arrange the categories in descending order of frequency or impact.
4. **Plot**: Draw the bar graph with categories on the horizontal axis and frequencies on the vertical axis.
5. **Add the Line**: Plot the cumulative percentage line to show the cumulative impact of each category.

### Purpose of a Pareto Chart:
- **Prioritization**: Helps identify the most significant issues or opportunities.
- **Focus Improvement Efforts**: Allows organizations to concentrate on the areas that will yield the greatest improvement.
- **Decision-Making**: Provides a clear visual representation to support decision-making processes.

- **Service Industry**: Determining the most frequent complaints or customer issues.
