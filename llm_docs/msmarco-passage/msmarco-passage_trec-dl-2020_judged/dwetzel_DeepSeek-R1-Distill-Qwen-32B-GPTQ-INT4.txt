1106979,define pareto chart in statistics,"A **Pareto chart** in statistics is a type of bar chart that displays the frequency or relative frequency of different categories or groups. It is named after the Italian economist Vilfredo Pareto, who observed that a small number of factors often account for a large proportion of the effect. The chart is used to identify the most significant factors contributing to a particular outcome or problem.

### Key Features of a Pareto Chart:
1. **Bar Chart**: The chart consists of vertical bars, where each bar represents a category or group. The height of the bar corresponds to the frequency, count, or percentage of occurrences for that category.
2. **Descending Order**: The bars are arranged in descending order of frequency, meaning the tallest bar is on the left, and the shortest is on the right.
3. **Cumulative Line**: A line graph is often overlaid on the bar chart to show the cumulative percentage of each category. This line helps to identify the cumulative contribution of the categories.

### Purpose of a Pareto Chart:
- To **prioritize** problems or opportunities by identifying the most significant factors.
- To **visualize** the Pareto Principle (also known as the 80/20 rule), which states that 80% of the effects come from 20% of the causes.
- To **guide decision-making** by highlighting areas that require the most attention or resources.

### Example:
Suppose a company wants to analyze the reasons for customer complaints. The Pareto chart might show that 80% of complaints are due to just 20% of the issues (e.g., product quality, delivery times). This helps the company focus on resolving the most impactful problems first.

### Steps to Create a Pareto Chart:
1. **Collect Data**: Gather data on the categories and their frequencies.
2. **Sort Data**: Arrange the categories in descending order of frequency.
3. **Calculate Cumulative Percentages**: Compute the cumulative percentage for each category.
4. **Plot the Chart**: Draw the bar chart with categories on the x-axis and frequencies on the y-axis. Add the cumulative line to show the running total.

Pareto charts are widely used in quality control, project management, and business analysis to identify and address the most critical issues efficiently."
