4. **Diabetes**: High blood sugar levels can damage blood vessels and lead to atherosclerosis, increasing the risk of stroke.

5. **High Cholesterol**: High levels of low-density lipoprotein (LDL) cholesterol can contribute to the buildup of plaques in the arteries.

6. **Smoking**: Smoking damages the blood vessels and can accelerate the hardening of arteries, increasing the risk of stroke.

7. **Obesity**: Being overweight or obese can increase the risk of stroke by contributing to high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and diabetes.

8. **Excessive Alcohol Consumption**: Drinking too much alcohol can raise blood pressure and increase the risk of stroke.

9. **Illegal Drug Use**: The use of drugs like cocaine and methamphetamine can increase the risk of stroke.

10. **Physical Inactivity**: Lack of physical activity can contribute to other risk factors for stroke, such as high blood pressure, high cholesterol, and diabetes.

11. **Age**: The risk of stroke increases with age, especially after age 55.

12. **Family History**: Having a family history of stroke increases your risk.

13. **Previous Stroke or Transient Ischemic Attack (TIA)**: If you've had a stroke or a TIA (a ""mini-stroke""), you're at higher risk of having another stroke.

Understanding these causes and risk factors can help in taking preventive measures and reducing the risk of stroke."
