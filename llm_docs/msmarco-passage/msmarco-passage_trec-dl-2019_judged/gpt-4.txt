207786,how are some sharks warm blooded,"Unlike most fish, some species of sharks, such as the salmon shark and the great white shark, have developed a mechanism called regional endothermic thermoregulation. This essentially enables them to retain the heat generated by their muscles from constant swimming, and to use that heat to warm their blood and vital organs.

This is achieved through a network of blood vessels in their bodies known as the rete mirabile, which translates to ""wonderful net"". When the warmer blood from the working muscles in their core passes through this network, it heats the colder blood coming in from the gills, which then circulates into the rest of the body.

This adaptation allows these types of sharks to venture into colder waters in search of prey, where other sharks or fish cannot survive. This gives them a significant advantage in terms of available habitats and food sources. 

However, it's important to remember that while these sharks are 'warm-blooded' in a sense, they are not homeothermic like mammals (maintaining a constant body temperature). The temperature of their body can still vary, although not as much as in other fishes."
