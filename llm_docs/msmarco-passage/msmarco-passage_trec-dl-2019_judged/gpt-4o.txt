   - **Totient \( \phi(n) \)**: Calculate the totient function \( \phi(n) = (p-1) \times (q-1) \).
   - **Public Exponent \( e \)**: Choose an integer \( e \) such that \( 1 < e < \phi(n) \) and gcd(\(e, \phi(n)\)) = 1; \( e \) is usually chosen as 65537 for efficiency, which is a common choice.
   - **Private Exponent \( d \)**: Compute \( d \) as the modular multiplicative inverse of \( e \) modulo \( \phi(n) \), i.e., \( d \equiv e^{-1} \pmod{\phi(n)} \).

2. **Public Key**:
   - Consists of the modulus \( n \) and the public exponent \( e \).
   - It is used for encrypting messages and verifying signatures.

3. **Private Key**:
   - Consists of the modulus \( n \) and the private exponent \( d \).
   - It is used for decrypting messages and signing messages.

4. **Encryption**:
   - A message \( M \) is encrypted using the public key \( (n, e) \) into ciphertext \( C \) using the formula:
     \[
     C \equiv M^e \pmod{n}
     \]

5. **Decryption**:
   - The ciphertext \( C \) is decrypted using the private key \( (n, d) \) back to the original message \( M \) using the formula:
     \[
     M \equiv C^d \pmod{n}
     \]

RSA’s security relies on the difficulty of factoring the large number \( n \). While it is computationally feasible to multiply large primes together, the reverse process, finding the prime factors of \( n \) (thus breaking the encryption), is hard and currently infeasible for sufficiently large values."
