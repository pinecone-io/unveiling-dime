490595,rsa definition key,"RSA is a public-key cryptosystem used for secure data transmission. It is named after its inventors, Ron Rivest, Adi Shamir, and Leonard Adleman, who published the algorithm in 1978. RSA is widely used for secure data transmission and is particularly important in the field of internet security.

### Key Components of RSA:

1. **Public Key**: This is a pair of numbers (n, e) that are used for encryption. The public key is shared with others and is used to encrypt messages.

2. **Private Key**: This is a pair of numbers (n, d) that are used for decryption. The private key must be kept secret and is used to decrypt messages that were encrypted with the corresponding public key.

### How RSA Works:

- **Key Generation**:
  - Choose two large prime numbers, p and q.
  - Compute n = p * q.
  - Compute the totient function φ(n) = (p-1)(q-1).
  - Choose an integer e such that 1 < e < φ(n) and gcd(e, φ(n)) = 1; that is, e and φ(n) are coprime.
  - Determine d as the modular multiplicative inverse of e modulo φ(n); that is, d is the number less than φ(n) such that (d * e) mod φ(n) = 1.

- **Encryption**:
  - To encrypt a message M (where M < n), compute the ciphertext C as C = M^e mod n.

- **Decryption**:
  - To decrypt the ciphertext C, compute the original message M as M = C^d mod n.

### Security of RSA:

The security of RSA is based on the difficulty of factoring the product of two large prime numbers. If an attacker can factor n into p and q, they can compute φ(n) and then d, which would allow them to decrypt messages. However, for sufficiently large values of n (typically 2048 bits or more), this is computationally infeasible with current technology.

RSA is used in many applications, including digital signatures, secure web browsing (HTTPS), and secure email."
